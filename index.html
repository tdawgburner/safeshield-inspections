Why Professional Testing Matters

The Critical Danger: Airborne Asbestos Fibers

Asbestos becomes dangerous when it becomes airborne. Intact asbestos materials pose minimal risk, but when disturbed during renovation, demolition, or repairs, microscopic fibers are released into the air where they can remain suspended for extended periods.

These invisible fibers are extremely small—much thinner than a human hair. Once airborne, they can:

  • Travel throughout your home via HVAC systems and air currents
  • Settle on furniture, carpets, and clothing where they can be disturbed repeatedly
  • Be inhaled deep into lung tissue
  • Contaminate areas far from the original disturbance
  • Remain a potential hazard for extended periods

Professional testing identifies these materials before any disturbance occurs, allowing for proper handling and containment procedures.

Federal & Virginia Regulatory Framework

EPA NESHAP (National Emission Standards for Hazardous Air Pollutants):

The EPA's asbestos NESHAP regulations apply to demolition and renovation activities. Key points include:

  • Inspection requirements before renovation or demolition in certain buildings
  • Notification requirements to regulatory authorities
  • Work practice standards for asbestos removal
  • Violations may result in significant penalties

Source: U.S. Environmental Protection Agency - Asbestos NESHAP
Visit: www.epa.gov/asbestos

Virginia Department of Environmental Quality (DEQ):

Virginia enforces asbestos regulations that may include state-specific requirements in addition to federal rules.

  • State notification and permitting requirements
  • Licensed asbestos professional requirements
  • Specific work practice standards

Source: Virginia Department of Environmental Quality
Visit: www.deq.virginia.gov

OSHA Worker Protection:

OSHA has standards to protect workers from asbestos exposure. Property owners should be aware that contractors working on their property must comply with OSHA requirements.

Source: OSHA Asbestos Standards
Visit: www.osha.gov/asbestos

Important: Regulations and enforcement vary by jurisdiction. Violations of asbestos regulations can result in substantial penalties, project delays, and legal liability.

Health Risks: Understanding Asbestos-Related Diseases

According to medical research and public health organizations, asbestos exposure is associated with serious health conditions with long latency periods.

  • Mesothelioma: A rare cancer primarily associated with asbestos exposure
  • Lung Cancer: Risk increases with asbestos exposure, particularly combined with smoking
  • Asbestosis: Chronic lung disease caused by inhaling asbestos fibers
  • Other conditions: Pleural thickening and other respiratory conditions

Sources: CDC, ATSDR, National Cancer Institute
Visit: www.cdc.gov/niosh/topics/asbestos

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does professional testing cost?

Inspections start at $250 for basic testing. Contact us for a free quote specific to your property size and scope.

How long does an inspection take?

Most residential inspections are completed within 1-2 hours. Laboratory analysis typically takes 3-5 business days, with expedited service available.

When was asbestos use discontinued?

Asbestos use was significantly reduced in the 1970s-80s but never fully banned. Homes built before 1980 are most likely to contain asbestos materials. Professional testing is the only way to know for certain.

What if asbestos is found in my home?

Finding asbestos doesn't necessarily mean immediate action is required. Intact materials may be left in place. Your report will include recommendations, which may include leaving in place, encapsulation, or removal by a licensed abatement contractor.

Is testing required for all renovation projects?

Requirements depend on scope of work and applicable regulations. Some renovations trigger mandatory testing under EPA and Virginia state rules. Even when not legally required, testing is strongly recommended to protect your family.
